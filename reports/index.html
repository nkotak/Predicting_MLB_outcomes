Against the betting market

On the 27,162 games with closing odds, the market's price (with the bookmaker's cut removed) scores a log loss of 0.6720 and the model scores 0.6762.

The gap is +0.0042, with a 95% range of +0.0032 to +0.0052, so the market is the better forecaster.

The learning agent (GRPO) is retrained before each season on earlier seasons only. In 9 of 9 seasons, no betting policy was proven better than not betting on the held-back season, so it passed on every game. Passing is a real result: it means the evidence did not support betting at these prices.

What the learning looked like: averaged over these seasons, the agent started out betting on 63% of its training games and ended up betting on 12%. On the held-back season, what it still wanted to bet on did not make money.

"Safety bar off" shows the bets the agent's learned policy would have placed if it were not required to prove itself first: 2,339 paper bets, 52.0% won, -4.5% return per unit staked (95% range -8.3% to -0.6%). The safety bar is what stopped those bets from being recommended.

How to read this page

No peeking. Every input for a game is built only from earlier games, and the model can only see an approved list of inputs. A test fails if any input changes when that game's own result is changed.

Calibration. When the model says 60%, it should happen about 60% of the time. The calibration error column shows how far off that is on average.

Why the market is hard to beat. Prices already reflect line-ups, injuries, weather and the money of professional bettors, and the bookmaker takes a cut of about 4% on top.
